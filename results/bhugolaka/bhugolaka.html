Bhūgolaka

What the word means across the library, and who held that the earth is a globe.

Jyotiṣa did, explicitly. From Āryabhaṭa on, the earth is a sphere, and the commentary argues against disc- and cart-shaped earths.

The word alone proves nothing. In the Bhāgavata and the Purāṇas that follow it, bhū-gola names the flat disc of dvīpas and oceans.

The globe travels. Kumārila, the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣikas, the Mokṣopāya/Yogavāsiṣṭha, Abhinavagupta and Kṣemarāja all use it.

1. Jyotiṣa: an explicit, argued sphere

Āryabhaṭīya, Golapāda 6–7

vṛttabhapañjaramadhye kakṣyāpariveṣṭitaḥ khamadhyagataḥ | mṛjjalaśikhivāyumayo bhūgolaḥ sarvato vṛttaḥ || 6 || yadvat kadambapuṣpagranthiḥ pracitaḥ samantataḥ kusumaiḥ | tadvad dhi sarvasattvair jalajaiḥ sthalajaiś ca bhūgolaḥ || 7 ||
8_jyot/aryabhata_aryabhatiya.txt:8092

The commentary (Shukla ed., INSA 1976)

The e-text merges Bhāskara I and Someśvara. The running commentary is taken here as Bhāskara I's; check against the print.

  • Rejects other shapes. bhuvaṃ tāvad anye śakaṭākārāṃ darpaṇavṛttākārāṃ ca manyante | naitad evam. Planets only look like discs because they are far away. :7850
  • Up and down are relative. sarveṣām asmadādīnāṃ bhūr adhaḥ, upari cākāśaḥ, with the kadamba verse quoted. :6745
  • Curvature. iyaṃ ca dharitrī golākārā paṭhyate … na caitāvatyā bhuvaḥ śakyate samīkaraṇaṃ kartum. The flat shadow-triangle fails on a curved surface of this size. :2844
  • Against Purāṇic geography. Rejects the dvīpa-doubling scheme and the 1000-yojana Gaṅgādvāra–Kumārī distance against measured distances (Laṅkā–Ujjayinī 200). Large Purāṇic figures are answered golākāratvād bhuvaḥ. :887
  • Rotation. The comment on Gola 9 first reads a rotating earth, then rejects it (oceans would flood, birds could not return to their nests) and reinterprets the verse. Which commentator wrote this layer is not settled. :8164

Sūryasiddhānta 12

  • 12.32: madhye samantād aṇḍasya bhūgolo vyomni tiṣṭhati, held by Brahmā's dhāraṇātmikā śakti.
  • 12.34–35: bhūgolamadhyago merur ubhayatra vinirgataḥ. Meru runs through the globe to both poles, gods above and asuras below. Purāṇic Meru fused into the sphere.
  • 12.53–54: sarvatraiva mahīgole svasthānam upari sthitam … alpakāyatayā lokāḥ … paśyanti vṛttām apy etāṃ cakrākārāṃ vasundharām. The round earth looks like a flat disc because we are small.

Lalla, Śiṣyadhīvṛddhida

  • Bhuvanakośa 17.5–8: golaḥ kādambo madhukarībhir iva sarvataḥ pracitaḥ. People on the underside are like a house-lizard on the ceiling beams (yathā gṛhagodhikā). na talam asti bhuvaḥ kva patatv asau: the earth has no ground to fall to.
  • A whole chapter of wrong views (20). v. 6: amitām avanīṃ pracakṣate susamāṃ kecana darpaṇopamām | apare … salilasthām atha yānapātravat.
  • Against the flat earth, vv. 34–37: yadi darpaṇavat samā mahī. Rain would run off sideways, and distant tall trees would never sink from sight. The earth looks mirror-flat only because the sky seems to meet it at the horizon.
  • He also refutes a tortoise or water as its support (39–41), the earth's rotation (42–43) and the Jainas' two suns (44).

Bhāskara II, Golādhyāya, Bhuvanakośa

bhūmeḥ piṇḍaḥ śaśāṅkajñakaviravikujejyārkinakṣatrakakṣā- vṛttair vṛto vṛtaḥ san mṛdanilasalilavyomatejomayo 'yam | nānyādhāraḥ svaśaktyaiva viyati niyataṃ tiṣṭhati … || 2 ||
  • Attraction, v. 6: ākṛṣṭiśaktiś ca mahī tayā yat khasthaṃ guru svābhimukhaṃ svaśaktyā | ākṛṣyate tat patatīva bhāti.
  • vv. 7–10: answers the Bauddhas (the earth falls forever, unnoticed, like a boat under its passengers) and the Jainas (two suns and moons).
  • Against the flat earth, vv. 11–12: yadi samā mukurodarasannibhā bhagavatī dharaṇī …. The Vāsanā names the opponent: purāṇe bhūḥ samādarśodarasannibhā kathyate. Munīśvara's Marīci quotes BhP 5.16.5 and 5.20.35 as that Purāṇic view.
  • Why it looks flat, v. 13: samo yataḥ syāt paridheḥ śatāṃśaḥ pṛthvī ca pṛthvī nitarāṃ tanīyān | naraś ca …. The Marīci adds Sūryasiddhānta 12.54.

2. Purāṇic bhūgola: the disc

  • Bhāgavata 5.16.4 opens its cosmography with bhū-golaka-viśeṣaṃ … vyākhyāsyāmaḥ. Then: Jambūdvīpa samavartulo yathā puṣkara-patram (5.16.5); Meru the pericarp of the earth-lotus (5.16.7); the golden land beyond ādarśa-talopamā (5.20.35); Lokāloka a quarter pañcāśat-koṭi-gaṇitasya bhū-golasya (5.20.38).
  • Devībhāgavata copies it: karṇikārūpa evāyaṃ bhūgolakamalasya ca. 3_purana/unknown_devibhagavatapurana.txt:27377
  • Nṛsiṃhapurāṇa 30 (bhūgolakathana) and Nāradapurāṇa 1.3 use bhūgola as the label for dvīpa cosmography. Jīva Gosvāmin calls BhP 5.16 the bhūgola-praśna.
  • The word does not occur in Viṣṇu, Vāyu or Matsya here. A guess, not verified: the Bhāgavata took a Jyotiṣa term for its own disc.
  • Partial absorption: Vahnipurāṇa explains night as the shadow of the sumeru-bhūgolaka and eclipses by golakacchāyā. 3_purana/unknown_vahnipurana.txt:2059
  • Kāvya mixes the images. Mālatīmādhava 5.22 namad-bhūgola presses on the cosmic tortoise; commentators gloss bhūmipiṇḍa, avanicakra, bhuvaḥ saṃsthānam. Gītagovinda 1.16: Varāha bhūgolam udbibhrate.

3. The globe outside Jyotiṣa

Mīmāṃsā

  • Kumārila, Tantravārttika on uttānā vai devagavā vahanti: pṛthivīgolaka-trailokya-bhramaṇādinā vā purāṇoktena dṛṣṭivaśād yathaiva vayam upari devān paśyāma … Our cows look upside-down to the gods. He ascribes the globe to purāṇa. 3_phil/mimamsa/kumarila_tantravarttika.txt:5677
  • Ajitā on the passage: nordhvādhobhāvo niyataḥ kintu ya eva yasyādhāraḥ tad eva tasyādhaḥ. 4_rellit/saiva/agama/unknown_ajita_adhyaya-1_iast.txt:12799 (TV with the Ajitā)
  • Śabara on MS 6.7.3: mṛdārabdhaṃ dravyāntaraṃ pṛthivīgolakam, na kṣetramātram. The whole earth as one substance; nothing about shape.

Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika: the largest earthy whole

  • Udayana: ā paramāṇor ā ca bhūgolakāt. 3_phil/vaisesik/udayana_kiranavali_dravya.txt:173
  • Śrīdhara: darkness as a substance would show its parts mahāndhakāre ca bhūgolakasyeva; the earth-globe shaken by adṛṣṭa. 3_phil/vaisesik/sridhara_nyayakandali_jetly-ed.txt:130
  • Gaṅgeśa's bhūgolakasantāna; Vyāsatīrtha's dvyaṇukādibhūgolakaparyanta; Jayatīrtha.
  • Bhaṭṭoji calls Udayana's ā ca bhūgolakam prāmādika; read ā bhūgolakāt. 1_gram/bhattoji-diksita_praudhamanorama.txt:2034

Mokṣopāya / Yogavāsiṣṭha

dharāgolakam antaḥsthasurāsuragaṇāspadam |veṣṭyate dhiṣṇyacakreṇa pakvākṣoṭam iva tvacā ||
Mokṣopāya 1.25.34 · 3_phil/vedanta/unknown_moksopaya.txt:6557

In YV Nirvāṇa-u. 127, the bhūgolanirṇaya, Rāma asks kathaṃ bhūgolakaṃ sthitam. Ānandabodhendra on v. 8 lists the vādins' answers:

  1. It falls forever through endless space, too large for us to notice; the star-wheel falls with it.
  2. It floats on an ocean like a boat, sinks at pralaya and rises like a gourd.
  3. Water all round; the wind in its hollow worlds keeps it rising.
  4. Only space all round; it always falls south, "down" from the gods on Meru.
  5. The āsura view: pātāla is up, so it falls north.
  6. The star-wheel stands still and the earth turns, as trees seem to move for people in a boat.
  7. Every side is "down" for those standing on it, so there is no direction to fall and it stays put.
3_phil/yogavasistha/unknown_yogavasistha_khanda-2_org.txt:69539–69715
  • Nirvāṇa-u. 131.7–17: beings move over it like ants on a ball (kanduka); the princes looking for the end of the earth keep circling.
  • Nirvāṇa-u. 205.14: antipodal peoples, ūrdhvakhurā lambamānavanācalāḥ.
  • Utpatti 30.12: ants on a vartulaloṣṭaka; the commentator notes this is jyotiḥśāstrakṛtām api saṃmataḥ. unknown_yogavasistha_utpattiprakarana.txt:9727

Kashmir Śaivism

  • Abhinavagupta, ĪPVV: the proud reasoner is like a fool who puts the pole star over the centre of the whole earth-globe as he imagines it from his own courtyard (svagṛhaprāṅgaṇamadhyakalpitaniḥśeṣabhūgolakamadhyadhruvakasthānavat). 4_rellit/saiva/trika/abhinavagupta_isvarapratyabhijnavivrtivimarsini_vol-3_m00022.txt:2412
  • Kṣemarāja on Svacchanda 10.511: Jyotiṣa's account of the stars bahirbhūgolam and the sizes of sun and earth holds for its own purpose, reading karma (quoting Laghujātaka 1.3). The Āgama's account is the real one, so na tena saha saṃvādavisaṃvādādicarcā kāryā. 4_rellit/saiva/unknown_svacchandatantra_w_ksemaraja-uddyota_m00091.txt:16378

4. The Bhāgavata commentators on 5.16–20

Source: the Kṛṣṇaśaṅkara Śāstrī edition of Skandha 5 with many commentaries (Ahmedabad 1965), PDF pp. 318–474 OCR'd with Google Vision. Printed here: Śrīdhara, Vaṃśīdhara, Vīrarāghava, Vijayadhvaja, Jīva, Viśvanātha, Śukadeva, Giridhara, Bhagavatprasāda. Page numbers are PDF pages.

Most take bhūgola as the disc

  • Śrīdhara, Vīrarāghava, Śukadeva, Giridhara and Bhagavatprasāda gloss bhūgola as the 50-koṭi bhūmaṇḍala and compute the belts. None of them raises Jyotiṣa.
  • samavartulaḥ (5.16.5) is glossed vartulatvena koṇābhāvāt sarvataḥ samaparimāṇaḥ: a circle without corners. pp. 331, 335
  • Vīrarāghava cuts the disc from Meru into four triangular sectors. p. 468

Gola as the egg-shell

  • Vijayadhvaja on 20.38: bhūgolakasya brahmāṇḍalakṣaṇasya. p. 464
  • Viśvanātha on 20.38: bhūgolakasya bhūsambandhāṇḍagolakasya … khagolam iva bhūgolam api pañcāśatkoṭiyojanapramāṇam. The earth inside is slightly smaller, so Śeṣa's bearing it and Varāha's lifting it make sense. p. 464

Harmonising Purāṇas, not Jyotiṣa

  • Vijayadhvaja on 16.7 overrides the Viṣṇupurāṇa's Meru with yathā bhāgavate tūktaṃ bhauvanaṃ kośalakṣaṇam | tasyāvirodhato yojyaṃ sarvagranthāntarasthitam. p. 332
  • Jīva on 16.6–29: ṭīkādarśitā matabhedāḥ sṛṣṭibhedena jñeyāḥ. p. 332
  • Jyotiṣa appears only as a calendar term: on 5.17.1 yuga is the five-year Jyotiṣa yuga (Vaṃśīdhara quoting Śrīpati; Jīva). pp. 350–351

Vaṃśīdhara on 5.20.38: the one real engagement

  • Quotes Nīlakaṇṭha (on the Bhīṣmaparvan): a square earth of side 4650 yojanas, roughly matching the jyotirvids' circumference of 4967. The sun circles Meru, so na bauddhābhimataṃ sūryadvayam; anyat sarvaṃ yuktyaviruddhaṃ jyotirvinmatam evātrānusartavyam. The Purāṇa's 50-koṭi bhūgola is acintya or scaled by 1/20.
  • His own: yojanas differ because hastas differ, so Jyotiṣa, Purāṇa and Itihāsa can be squared. Neither side overrides the other. Vyāsa only seems to contradict Jyotiṣa, asurajanakartṛkaśāstrapravṛttisaṃkocārtham.
  • The Golādarśa solution: two earths. bhūgolakapadena jyotiḥśāstrokto golākāro mārttiko 'ṇḍaḥ | viśeṣapadena maṇḍalākāraḥ purāṇokto hiraṇmayo 'ṇḍaḥ. He finds the globe in the Bhāgavata itself (Śeṣa holds the earth siddhārtha iva; prekṣayitvā bhuvo golam; 10.8.37). The big Purāṇic earth is a magnified reflection above the stars, cast by a water- or fire-sphere that works dūrakāntavat, apparently like a telescope (citing the Puliśasiddhānta and Sūryasiddhānta).
  • He closes by preferring his own earlier solution: etadvirodhaparihāro yaḥ pūrvam eva mayālekhi sa eva samīcīnaḥ.
